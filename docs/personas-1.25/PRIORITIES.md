# Findings to address after the usage study

The annual cohort is fictional. “Confirmed” below means a current rule or reproducible code behavior, not a finding from customer interviews. No app rules were changed during the study.

| ID | Priority / type | Evidence | Next concrete improvement | Existing roadmap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U01 | Highest — confirmed defect | Jordan earns Network Weaver but its commissions are classified as profession work by the `network-` prefix. Class level stays 1. The supplementary probe finds this scope mismatch in the 51-class catalogue. | Assign scope from explicit request ownership. Protect class IDs from technical-request filters. Test daily/weekly/monthly class work, a later profession/vocation change, and retained accepted requests. Repair historical class credit without subtracting already earned rewards. | P01/P09 |
| U02 | High — progression UX | Theo reaches player 69 and vocation 108 while class/profession stop at 4. The matched review run has the same work, XP and gold but Scholar 20 / Data Analyst 104. Nadia reaches player 61 / profession 97 while Sentinel stays 1 because the schedule includes no class work. | Show separate **XP banked**, **class work needed**, **exam ready**, and **next qualifying action** states. Offer direct actions on the Guild hub and in Help; retain manual choice and the existing exam gates. | P03/P04/P09 |
| U03 | High — vertical lineage design | Leila ends Engineer 55 but retains Artificer 4. Grand Artificer specifically requires Artificer 25. Similarly, Minstrel/Duelist/Tactician levels are not interchangeable with named basic/prestige predecessors. | Decide which related advanced classes should satisfy a lineage or alternative trial. Show the path before a switch. Preserve old levels; do not silently change earned eligibility. Some prestige paths also lack a direct Ascendant successor. | P01/P02/P09 |
| U04 | High — reward/schedule balance | Primary outcomes range from about 173 to 1,904 XP per assigned real-world hour. Low-budget split-focus profiles miss more calendar thresholds; 995 accepted commitments expire across 35 scenarios. This is not a controlled occupation comparison. | Run matched effort/frequency tests with identical time budgets. Add attainable lower-frequency quest options and a feasibility preview based on remaining days and recovery. Review novice mental/career difficulty alongside the F–C physical ladder. Keep deliberate acceptance. | P08/P09 |
| U05 | High — career/stat consistency | The profession daily request is INT for every tested role; 20 of 32 role setups have a different career-category focus stat. The category still determines permanent profession milestone gains. | Author focus from the actual activity and use the category as a documented fallback. INT may be correct for a trade-learning task, but the difference should be intentional and visible. Review profession-specific work-sample options for DEX/CON categories such as trades, hospitality, manufacturing and logistics. | P07/P09 |
| U06 | Medium — vocation/eligibility semantics | Quinn's software work contributes 70,020 XP to the selected UX Designer profession, while technology and creative trees remain separate. Engineer checks the profession category and ignores the software vocation for that gate. | Decide whether cross-category vocation work should credit that profession, and whether vocation mastery offers alternate class eligibility. Label destinations clearly. Profession selection may be aspirational; do not imply that a label verifies employment or licensure. | P07/P09 |
| U07 | Medium — later career depth | Maya, Priya and Noah have bought all 12 current modern nodes in their sampled career sets but retain 727, 925 and 979 career points respectively. Other sampled wallets have no currently learnable node despite spare points. | Deepen the maintained 20 trees with useful later competencies, work-sample goals and additional non-combat applications. Expand beyond the networking-only credential vocabulary. The detailed role assignments in the profiles were authored for this study; the app does not supply 32 complete curricula. Broaden job-search guidance beyond the Analyst-specific branch using an explicit job-search goal, rather than assuming a title. Do not add dozens of shallow role-specific trees or remove earned points. | P01/P07/P08 |
| U08 | Medium — partial work and plateau support | Paige's two unfinished workouts earn no receipt and do not become independent Journey entries. The isolated fixed 0–100 rubric probe cannot pass from a baseline already at 100; mastery still requires two qualifying improvements. | Add independent partial-session records without full completion rewards. Provide clear alternate/harder comparable assessments and explain existing mastery requirements; preserve the owner-approved improvement requirement. Do not infer failure from a maintained standard alone. | P04/P09 |
| U09 | Medium — ordinary Adventure challenge | All 5,543 selected ordinary encounters are won. Nadia's career-focused, class-level-1 hero can keep progressing with companions. The shared bot uses a limited tactic policy and chooses no Elite hunts. | Preserve companion-led play. Test optional challenge recommendations, varied enemy patterns and later career tool use against broader policies. This result does not justify mandatory grinding, companion nerfs or per-encounter recovery. | P02/P08 |
| U10 | Before broad release — observed usability | The scenario planner knows requirements and budget allocations; real users may not discover them. No native, retention or efficacy inference follows from these simulations. | Pilot with a nurse, tradesperson, teacher, low-time driver, career changer, older RPG beginner and enthusiastic gamer. Observe task choice, expiry understanding, exams, class switching and perceived relevance. Keep P05 device checks and deferred Google setup visible. | P05/P09 |

The app's breadth is a strength: all 20 category utilities are reached, and the 32 profiles use 28 class definitions without seeding progression. Later depth, meaningful prerequisite paths and readable progression states are stronger next investments than simply adding more profession names.
