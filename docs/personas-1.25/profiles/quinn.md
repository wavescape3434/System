# Quinn Harper — UX Designer

**Fictional customer; scripted app outcomes. The experience assessment below is an inference, not a testimonial.**

## Background

Quinn is a designer learning to build small prototypes. Their profession and vocation intentionally belong to different categories. They want the app to respect that distinction and retain both kinds of work. Engineer is an attractive class name, but its profession-category gate may not accept a design profession even when software practice has advanced.

## Goals and intended use

- **Scenario career output:** A fictional usability flow plus a separately tested implementation.
- **Learning focus:** Usability and tested interactive prototypes.
- **Class ambition:** Engineer. The chosen class is independent of the profession.
- **Constraints:** Cross-category profession/vocation pairing; does not silently relabel the profession to qualify for Engineer.

## Experience assessment

A software vocation can earn technology-tree progress while the selected profession remains UX Designer. Engineer still checks the profession category. The same vocation receipts also add XP to the current profession, so credit destinations need clearer design and labeling rather than an unexplained locked-class message.

The app credited **70,020 XP** from technology-category profession-scope work to the selected UX Designer profession. The Engineer profession gate still rejects the creative category. The selection and credit rules should be explained together; the app permits aspirational profession choices.

Current aspiration: **Engineer**. Remaining requirements: Profession: Engineering & Architecture / Computing & Networks / Construction & Skilled Trades; Engineer entry trial.
